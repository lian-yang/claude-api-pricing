Kling 3.0 pricing — plans, credits and real cost per video

Kling 3.0 doesn't have a per-video price — it bills per second, in credits. Here's what that means for a 5-second clip, which plan actually fits your volume, and the free-tier reality.

What Kling 3.0 costs per video

Kling 3.0 draws ~6 credits per second at 720p without audio, and ~12 credits per second at 1080p with native audio. The math for common lengths:

Third-party API resellers (kie.ai and similar) quote their own per-video rates that can undercut app credits for bulk pipelines.

Is Kling 3.0 free?

Not in any unlimited sense. The app grants free trial credits at signup plus occasional promo/login credits — enough for a handful of short test clips at normal credit rates. For regular use you need a plan or top-ups. If you see a "free Kling 3.0 generator" site, it's reselling API access behind ads or its own credits.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Kling 3.0 cost?
Per-second in credits: ~6 credits/s at 720p (no audio), ~12 credits/s at 1080p (with audio). A 5-second 720p clip is ~45 credits ≈ $0.68 at the ~$1/66-credit list rate, less inside discounted plans.
What are Kling's subscription plans?
Standard $6.99 first month then ~$8.80/mo (660 credits), Pro ~$25.99/mo (~3,000 credits), and higher tiers up to ~4,500 credits with 1080p, audio sync and commercial rights. Monthly plans cancel anytime.
Is there a free tier?
Only trial/promo credits — a few short clips' worth. There's no unlimited free generation; recurring use needs a plan or credit top-ups.
Do failed generations cost credits?
In many cases yes — moderation rejections usually don't, but failed renders on certain prompts can still draw credits. Budget ~10–20% for retries on experimental prompts.
Kling 1.5 vs 3.0 — what actually changed?
3.0 adds native audio generation, 4K-class output, multi-shot generation and markedly better motion realism and prompt adherence. Kling 1.5 is the legacy generation: 1080p, weakest at complex scenes and camera work, but it bills at the cheapest legacy credit rates. If a clip is silent and simple, 1.5 still works; anything audience-facing benefits from 3.0.
Kling 2.6 vs 3.0 — which should I use?
2.6 is the cost/quality sweet spot for silent clips at volume; 3.0 is the flagship with synced audio, multi-shot and top realism. Expect a 3.0 clip with 1080p + audio to draw roughly 2–5x the credits of a 2.x-era equivalent. Rule of thumb: volume-silent → 2.5/2.6; deliverable-quality with audio → 3.0.
Kling 3 vs Kling 3 Omni — what do I pay extra for?
Omni builds on 3.0 with stronger reference workflows — feeding multiple reference images for consistent characters and scenes across shots — plus native audio tooling. Standard 3.0 is the base flagship; Omni is aimed at character-driven, reference-based production work.
