The positioning story: flagship prices after a budget era

K3's $3/$15 put it in the same ballpark as GPT-5.6 Terra ($2/$12) — and sparked genuine debate among developers who came to Kimi precisely because K2 was cheap. The honest read: K3 is a flagship-tier bid (1M context included), not a budget play. For budget workloads the K2 line at ~$0.95/$4 is still there, and DeepSeek V4.1-Flash at $0.30/$1.20 undercuts everything.

Cache economics: where K3 actually wins

Cached input at $0.30/M is a 90% discount. On workloads that re-send long prompts — agent loops, document Q&A, codebase chat — the input side of the bill collapses. Worked example at 30M input + 5M output/day: full-fresh costs $165/day (~$4,950/mo), but with 75% cache hits the same volume runs ~$104/day (~$3,125/mo) — a 37% cut from caching alone.

Kimi K3 vs Claude

At list, K3 ($3/$15) is pricier than Claude Sonnet 5.5 ($2/$10) on both axes and cheaper than Opus 5.5's $20 output. The structural case for K3 is the 1M context (vs Claude's 200K default) — entire codebases in one call. The structural case against: for most sub-200K tasks, Sonnet 5.5 is both cheaper and adjacent in quality; for volume tasks, K2.6 or DeepSeek V4.1-Flash cost 4–12x less.

Cheaper than list: the aggregator route

OpenRouter lists K3 at roughly $0.25 input / $14.23 output — batching discounts make the input side dramatically cheaper than the official $3. If your workload tolerates batch latency, that's the play; real-time product features stay on the official platform.
