Two honest readings. Short-context workloads (most agentic runs): Sol is half of Opus everywhere — list, batch and cached input all line up at exactly 2x. Where each model earns its keep

  • Sol's case: near-Astra agentic capability at half of Opus list and a quarter of Opus batch; the default for code review, document work and high-volume agents; ~3x faster Codex completion than Astra in independent testing.
  • Opus 5.5's case: Anthropic's flagship reasoning tier with a full-price-free 1M window — the structural pick for sustained >272K workloads, plus whatever edge your own evals measure on long-run agent coherence.
  • The honest tiebreak: price is settled (50% saved at list); capability is workload-specific. Run both on your 20 hardest tasks, then split traffic. Cross-vendor alternatives in the same price band: DeepSeek V4 vs Opus, Argon vs Opus.

Frequently asked questions

Is GPT-6.1 Sol cheaper than Claude Opus 5.5?
Yes, at every lever: half at list ($2/$10 vs $4/$20), a quarter at batch ($1/$5 vs $2/$10), half on cached input ($0.10 vs $0.20). A 1M-in/100K-out daily workload runs $180/mo on Sol vs $360/mo on Opus.
Which is smarter — Sol or Opus 5.5?
No official head-to-head exists: OpenAI benchmarks Sol against its own Astra flagship, not Opus. Opus 5.5 is Anthropic's flagship reasoning tier. Settle it with your own eval — the price half of the decision is already settled in Sol's favor.
Why is GPT-6.1 Sol cheaper than the old GPT-5.6 Sol?
One generation apart: GPT-5.6-era Sol ran $4/$20 promotional (from $5/$30); GPT-6.1 Sol launches at $2/$10 with $1/$5 batch. Check your model string before comparing quotes — "Sol" now means two different price points.
Does Sol cost more above 272K context?
Yes — $4/$15 per million (output +50%), charged per-request past the 272K input boundary. Opus 5.5 has no surcharge to 1M, which narrows the gap on long-context workloads to roughly 1.3-2.7x depending on mix — Opus's single structural price win.
