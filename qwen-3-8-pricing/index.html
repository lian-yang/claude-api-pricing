Qwen 3.8 API pricing — $2/$6 flagship, with a $0.15 budget line

Positioning: flagship output at Sonnet-money input

Qwen3.8-Max was released Aug 2, 2026 as the family's most capable model, pitched at coding and agentic "cowork" work. The price cuts an unusual path: it matches Claude Sonnet 5.5's $2 input but charges $6 output vs Sonnet's $10, and lands between GPT-5.6 Terra ($2/$12) and Kimi K3 ($3/$15). Against Opus 5.5 ($4/$20) it's 2x cheaper in, 3.3x cheaper out. The budget floor still belongs to DeepSeek V4.1-Flash ($0.15–0.30/$0.60–1.20), with Qwen3.8-Flash ($0.15/$0.47) the closest challenger.

Cache economics: the 10% rate does the heavy lifting

Alibaba bills implicit cache hits at 10% of input price — $0.20/M on Max. Worked example at 30M input + 5M output/day on Max: full-fresh runs $90/day (~$2,700/mo); at 75% cache hits the input side drops to $19.50 and the same volume costs $49.50/day (~$1,485/mo) — a 45% cut from caching alone. The same workload on Sonnet 5.5 is $110/day fresh, so Max wins both with and without cache. For per-request math, the token calculator covers the Claude side.

The reasoning_effort bill you didn't see

Qwen3.8 exposes a reasoning_effort parameter — and reviewers have noted it defaults to overthinking, burning thinking tokens that bill as output at $6/M on Max. For cost control: set reasoning to low or medium on routine calls, reserve high-effort mode for the slices that need it, and route everything else to Flash, where the same overthinking costs $0.47/M instead.

Lineup gotchas worth knowing

Three things the pricing page won't flag: qwen3.8-plus doesn't exist (the Plus line tops out at Qwen3.7-Plus), there's no qwen3.8-max-preview either (that slot is qwen3.7-max-preview at $2.5/$7.5, thinking-only), and unlike the 3.5/3.6/3.7 lines, the 3.8 listings carry no long-context surcharge — flat rates to 1M-token prompts where older Flash tiers rise up to 4x past 128K.

Hosting alternatives: the Cerebras route for 27B

The open-weight models aren't locked to Alibaba. Cerebras hosts Qwen3.8-27B at $0.99/$1.49 per million — pricier input than Alibaba's $0.50, but less than half the output price and pitched on inference speed. Self-hosting the 27B is realistic on a single high-memory GPU; the 2.4T-A95B MoE is a datacenter-scale job. Community quantized builds for local runs are active.
