Gemini 4 Hydrogen API pricing — $1.50 input, $7.50 output, flat to 1M tokens

Google's new fast agentic model (launched Nov 12, 2026) prices gemini-4-hydrogen at $1.50 / $7.50 per MTok with no long-context surcharge across the full 1M-token window. It replaces Gemini 3.8 Flash at 2.5x the task speed, serves as the default model for AI Mode in Search and Antigravity agents — and its list price equals exactly what 3.8 Flash itself rises to on Jan 1, 2027. All rates below are from Google's announcement and the OpenRouter model page.

FAQ

How much does Gemini 4 Hydrogen cost?

$1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens (model ID gemini-4-hydrogen), verified Nov 13, 2026 against Google's Nov 12 announcement and the OpenRouter model page. Pricing is flat across the full 1M-token context; cache reads are $0.075/MTok; the batch variant halves both lines to $0.75/$3.75.

Is Gemini 4 Hydrogen replacing Gemini 3.8 Flash?

Yes — rolled out Nov 12, 2026 as the 3.8 Flash replacement with a claimed 2.5x faster task completion, and it's the default model for AI Mode in Search and for Antigravity agents. Its $1.50/$7.50 rates equal exactly what 3.8 Flash rises to on Jan 1, 2027 (from $0.75/$3.75), so migrating Flash workloads before January avoids paying Hydrogen's price for Flash's speed.

Gemini 4 Hydrogen vs Gemini 4 Argon — which should you use?

Hydrogen ($1.50/$7.50) is the fast agentic tier; Argon ($2/$10 intro through Nov 30, then $4/$20) is the hard-reasoning flagship. Hydrogen is cheaper on both lines and faster per task; Argon is the pick when reasoning depth, not speed, is the constraint.

Does the price go up on long contexts?

No. Google's published pricing is flat across the entire 1M-token context window — no surcharge tiers like some competitors' long-context schemes. Cache reads at $0.075/MTok are the main lever for workflows that re-read large contexts.

Is there a free tier for Gemini 4 Hydrogen?

Launch pricing material didn't publish a free tier. Check the official AI Studio pricing page for the current free-quota policy — Google has historically run no-cost quotas for new Gemini models, but we don't list unverified rates.
